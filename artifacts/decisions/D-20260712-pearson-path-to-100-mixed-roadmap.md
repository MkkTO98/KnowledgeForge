# D-20260712 Pearson Path-to-100 Mixed Roadmap

Date: 2026-07-12
Status: accepted

## Decision
Adopt disposition E for Pearson production sequencing: use a mixed production roadmap instead of treating the raw-level Pearson path-to-100 object-count target as the primary operational success measure.

Preserve accepted raw Pearson packages as immutable canonical knowledge. Continue raw Pearson only as a limited baseline descriptor and cautionary relationship family, while prioritizing bounded candidate-policy refinement, transformation-aware deterministic relationships, and richer deterministic diagnostics before further large raw-level expansion.

## Evidence
- Accepted Pearson objects reviewed: 21.
- Campaign 41 accepted objects: 8.
- Campaign 41 high shared-time-trend risk: 8 / 8.
- Numeric time-index diagnostics available across Pearson objects: 14 / 21.
- Numeric time-index diagnostics lacking: 7 / 21.
- Review-only high time-risk among numeric-diagnostic objects: 14 / 14.
- Review-only strong raw plus weak first-difference pattern: 5 objects.
- Semantic proximity distribution: 12 close, 1 moderate, 8 remote.
- Campaign 41 cross-family diversity produced semantically remote pairs that are more useful as cautionary/baseline relationship knowledge than as positive explanatory relationship knowledge.

## Rationale
KnowledgeForge should optimize for reusable, objective, reproducible knowledge value, not relationship-object count. Raw Pearson remains useful as a deterministic baseline descriptor, but the current 21-object evidence materially shows that large raw-level expansion risks accumulating misleadingly strong shared-time-trend relationships unless paired with semantic screening, time-risk stratification, and transformation-aware companion methods.

This decision does not reject Campaign 41. Campaign 41 is technically successful and remains accepted. It instead changes the sequencing interpretation of the roadmap.

## Rejected alternatives
- A: Continue raw-level Pearson production toward 100 unchanged. Rejected because Campaign 41 and the 21-object inventory show object-count growth would likely outpace reusable knowledge value.
- B: Continue raw-level Pearson production with stronger screening only. Partially included, but insufficient by itself because transformation-aware work is now needed before large raw expansion.
- C: Pause raw expansion and validate transformation-aware Pearson only. Too narrow; raw Pearson remains valuable as a limited baseline/cautionary family.
- D: Prioritize another deterministic family only. Too broad a pivot; Pearson evidence specifically supports a mixed relationship roadmap.
- F: Preserve existing roadmap due to insufficient evidence. Rejected because 21 objects, including 8/8 high-risk Campaign 41 results, are sufficient production evidence for sequencing change.

## Architectural classification
Roadmap sequencing decision within existing architecture.

No Production Doctrine amendment, KnowledgeObjectPackage redesign, PostgreSQL schema expansion, method implementation, canonical package mutation, or new coefficient/package production is authorized by this decision.
