# Candidate-Construction Policy Assessment

## Campaign 41 selector assessment

Campaign 41's deterministic selector served reusable discovery partially: it proved that a frozen declarative batch can produce, publish, retrieve, and export multiple candidate relationships without coefficient-dependent selection. However, its cross-family diversity preference optimized diversity too strongly for reusable knowledge value.

Evidence:

- Campaign 41 accepted objects: 8 / 8.
- Campaign 41 numeric high shared-time-trend risk: 8 / 8.
- Campaign 41 semantically remote objects: 8 / 8 under the review rubric.
- Multiple strong raw associations weaken sharply in first differences, e.g. SWE private credit x mobile subscriptions and SWE private credit x internet users.

## Policy finding

The policy likely systematically creates high-risk shared-trend pairs when it prioritizes cross-family diversity without a bounded semantic-risk screen. This is not a correctness defect in Campaign 41 and must not retroactively alter accepted packages.

## Recommended bounded adjustment

Before further raw-level expansion, add a sequencing gate that requires candidate registry reports to stratify semantic proximity and expected time-risk from metadata/known diagnostics before coefficient calculation. This should be a candidate-policy refinement, not a method implementation and not a package redesign.

Local AI was not used. Deterministic metadata and manual rubric were sufficient; no new local-AI infrastructure was justified.
