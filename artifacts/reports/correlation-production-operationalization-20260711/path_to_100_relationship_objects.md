# Realistic Path to 100 Substantive Relationship Objects

Current Pearson relationship objects: 7. Additional objects needed for 100: 93.

Viable path without broad maps or coefficient-driven selection:

1. Maintain one declarative spec per bounded batch and run the generic engine for no-promote reproduction.
2. Use coefficient-free candidate triage by verified Mature family, unit clarity, non-identity construction, coverage >= 0.85, variance, and reusable semantic category.
3. Batch size: 5-8 pairs per campaign after maturation assessment, not 1-3, with a single consolidated selection decision.
4. Expected rejection allowance: assume 20-35% selection-stage rejection and 5-10% post-freeze construction rejection.
5. Required gross candidate pool: roughly 130-150 coefficient-free candidates to net 93 additional accepted objects.
6. Operational campaign count: about 14-19 batches at 5 accepted objects/batch, or 10-13 batches at 8 accepted objects/batch.
7. Do not scale by all-pairs maps. Scale by reusable semantic templates: coverage-vs-coverage, rate-vs-rate, access-vs-subscription, stock-vs-flow where non-identity, environmental intensity pairs, agriculture production/land-use pairs, education enrollment transitions, and health-service coverage pairs.
8. Stop gates: rising governance ratio above 9:1, repeated post-freeze rejection, PostgreSQL retrieval friction, or evidence families with unclear units/provenance.

Verdict: 100 relationship objects is realistic only after Pearson-family maturation and a spec-driven candidate registry. It is not realistic through bespoke campaign runners.
