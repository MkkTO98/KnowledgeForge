# Evidence-Family Prioritization Report — Phase 2

Date: 2026-07-09
Status: completed planning artifact

## Evaluation criteria

Candidate families were evaluated by:

- reuse of existing methodology;
- production value as objective knowledge;
- deterministic suitability;
- validator coverage;
- remaining falsification gaps exercised;
- knowledge reuse potential;
- dependency relationships;
- long-term scalability;
- constitutional boundary risk.

Economic or investment importance was not used as a primary ranking criterion.

## Recommended ordering rationale

Infrastructure should start Phase 2 because it maximizes continuity with the Campaign 8 selection evidence while still adding a third distinct WDI annual-scalar family.

Energy & Mining should follow because it is still controlled in size and source shape, but introduces more technical indicator semantics.

Agriculture should follow as a smaller-family contrast. If closeout remains efficient here, it strengthens confidence that the maturity doctrine is not dependent on large family breadth.

After three or four families, WDI cross-family comparison should become more valuable than another isolated family because it exercises multi-reference, overlap, duplicate, and provenance-difference behaviour across a broader evidence base.

Trade and Financial Sector are valuable after boundary behaviour is further proven because they raise the risk of unsupported economic meaning. They should not be avoided permanently; they should be sequenced after lower-risk families.

Education and Health should be used as scale tests after the method has shown stable behaviour across additional moderate/small families.

Non-WDI multi-source disagreement is the highest falsification value, but it should be deliberately gated after WDI breadth makes internal methodology stability less ambiguous.

## Architectural continuity conclusion

All prioritization recommendations preserve the agreed architecture. No recommendation requires refinement, duplicate concept creation, contradiction of accepted decisions, or architectural drift.
